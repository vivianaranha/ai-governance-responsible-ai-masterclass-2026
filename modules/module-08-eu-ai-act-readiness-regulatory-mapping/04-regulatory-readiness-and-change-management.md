# Tutorial 08.4 — Regulatory Readiness and Change Management

**Created by School of AI**

**Module:** EU AI Act Readiness & Regulatory Mapping

## Governance objective

Understand how **regulatory readiness and change management** becomes an operational governance decision, control or evidence requirement.

## 1. Identify the governed object

Start with the actual AI system or capability:
- purpose
- owner
- users
- affected people
- model(s)
- data
- retrieval
- tools/agents
- deployment environment
- third parties
- jurisdictions

Do not govern a vague label such as "GenAI" when the real risks exist in a specific use case and lifecycle.

## 2. State the risk

A useful governance risk statement includes:
- event or failure,
- cause,
- affected party,
- consequence,
- existing controls,
- residual concern.

## 3. Convert risk into controls

Controls should identify:
- owner,
- enforcement point,
- frequency,
- test method,
- evidence,
- escalation path.

A policy with no control owner or evidence path is not enough.

## 4. Define the decision gate

Possible outcomes include:
- approve,
- approve with conditions,
- remediate,
- escalate,
- suspend,
- reject,
- retire.

High-risk systems should require stronger evidence and more independent challenge.

## 5. Preserve traceability

Maintain links:

**System → Risk → Control → Test → Evidence → Decision → Reviewer → Date**

This is what makes governance auditable and repeatable.

Regulatory readiness means mapping actual system roles and use contexts to current obligations. Never assume a regulation applies identically to every AI system, entity or jurisdiction.

## Practical exercise

Create one governance record containing:
1. system/use case,
2. risk,
3. risk tier,
4. required control,
5. owner,
6. evidence,
7. review decision,
8. reassessment trigger.
